A NOTE ON THE METHOD

Evidence before a headline.

01 / COLLECT

Start at the source.

We check official documents, audits, court records, procurement notices and archives, alongside local reporting. The desk covers 26 countries, with deeper attention to Kenya, Tanzania, Malawi, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Botswana and South Africa.

02 / COMPARE

Ask what is actually new.

Potential leads are checked against earlier coverage, including relevant local-language searches. Translating someone else’s investigation does not make it an exclusive. No search results is not proof that nobody has reported a story.

03 / VERIFY

Keep the gaps in view.

Documents have page or paragraph references. Allegations, findings and reporting questions remain distinct. Private security, military contractors and state forces are different categories. Historical accounts need context and corroboration.

04 / REPORT

A lead needs reporting.

These files are research directions, not finished investigations or publication-ready findings. Originality is unconfirmed unless stated and supported. Source responses, independent checks and editorial review are still required.

When does the desk update?

The research workflow is scheduled for 7 a.m. Eastern. Completion time varies, and local research requires the computer and Codex to be available. This site updates when a completed briefing is published from that workflow. It is not a live news feed. The displayed research date is the freshness indicator.

Browsing and public-source collection use the existing Codex setup without an additional paid search API. Some sources block collection or need a dedicated reader. Those gaps remain visible rather than being treated as “no news.”
